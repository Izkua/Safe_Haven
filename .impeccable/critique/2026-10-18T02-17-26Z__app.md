## Design Specificity Verdict

**LLM assessment:** the system itself is specific: dark grey by default, warm beige for light mode, color only for signals, and a real urgency ladder on the alert screen. But several structures are the default shapes an AI reaches for, and they make the app read as generic:

1. **Onboarding hero template.** Every step is a huge icon above a bold title above a grey paragraph above stacked buttons, with five progress dots. It's the most common AI-generated onboarding, and the icons are left-aligned, which makes it look unfinished.
2. **Symmetric tile pair.** "I'm safe" and "I need help" are identical tonal tiles, icon over label, side by side. Equal weight is the AI default for two choices. In an emergency app the help call should outrank the reassurance.
3. **Middot chains.** The `·`-joined strings ("Earthquake · Emergency · just now", "Safe · 3 min ago", "340 m · 5 min walk") are an AI copy tic. One or two are fine; on every line they read as machine-written.
4. **Uniform rounded-rect everything.** Every action is the same 56pt, 12px-radius, 17/600 button. Hierarchy comes only from fill color, which is accepted, but the Map sheet still reads as a generic button stack.
5. **Settings-list drill picker.** Six equal rows, each with a red icon and a chevron: the generic iOS settings list, with color that doesn't mean anything.

**Deterministic scan:** not applicable (native platform).

## Overall Impression

It's calm, readable and buttons look like buttons. But it isn't **alarming** anywhere except inside the alert modal. Close that modal, or have a family member press "Need help", and the rest of the app goes back to looking peaceful. For an emergency app, the danger state has to follow you everywhere. That's the single biggest opportunity.

## What's Working

- **The urgency ladder on the alert screen.** An amber header, then a red header, then full red, with matching haptics and press-and-hold to dismiss. It's specific, escalates honestly and is hard to dismiss by accident.
- **Ask-first disclosure on Group.** Forms only appear after you tap their button, which keeps the screen readable.
- **Earned color in the palette.** There's no pure white and no decorative color, and everything passes at least 4.5:1 contrast in both appearances.

## Priority Issues

1. **[P1] Danger disappears outside the alert modal.**
   - **Why it matters:** someone who taps "Go to…" or the back gesture lands on a calm map while the earthquake is still active. A "Sis needs help" status is only visible if you open the Group tab or happen to spot her pin.
   - **Fix:** a persistent red banner at the top of the Map whenever an alert is active ("Earthquake warning: see what to do"), plus one when any member needs help. A red badge on the Group tab when anyone needs help.
   - **Suggested command:** /impeccable bolder

2. **[P1] Red is spent on things that aren't danger.**
   - **Why it matters:** "Practice a disaster" on the Map is red, and all six hazard icons on the drill picker are red. That teaches users that red means "a feature" and blunts the real alarms.
   - **Fix:** make drills neutral (plain text in the text color, neutral icons). Reserve red for live danger and the help action.
   - **Suggested command:** /impeccable quieter

3. **[P1] "I need help" has the same weight as "I'm safe".**
   - **Why it matters:** the most important button in the app, when you need it, is a tonal tile identical to the reassurance tile.
   - **Fix:** give "I need help" a red outline at rest, so it reads as the alarm before it's pressed, with a full red fill when active. Keep "I'm safe" tonal.
   - **Suggested command:** /impeccable bolder

4. **[P2] The onboarding hero template looks AI-generated and unfinished.**
   - **Why it matters:** first impressions decide trust, and left-aligned 72pt glyphs over left text look like a placeholder.
   - **Fix:** center the icon and the copy as one block, and size the icon down to 64pt so the title leads.
   - **Suggested command:** /impeccable onboard

5. **[P2] Middot chains in the copy.**
   - **Why it matters:** they're hard to scan under stress and read as machine copy.
   - **Fix:** use natural phrasing: "340 m away, about 5 min walk", "Safe, 3 min ago", and alert meta "Earthquake, just now".
   - **Suggested command:** /impeccable clarify

## Persona Red Flags

- **Jordan (first-timer, an older relative):** after an alert they tap "Go to Husky Stadium" and land on the Map with no reminder of what's happening or what to do next. The red drill link makes them wonder whether something is wrong right now.
- **Casey (one-handed, moving):** to ask for help from the Map, they must switch to the Group tab, find "How are you?" and tap. There's no help action in the Map's thumb zone. The danger banner (P1) should sit where it's visible, and help should be reachable fast.
- **Sam (screen reader):** status rows announce correctly. But the active-alert state isn't announced anywhere after the modal closes. The new banner must use an alert role.

## Minor Observations

- Appearance settings live under the First aid tab. Fine for now, but label the section "Settings" so it's findable.
- The Map can stack up to four top banners (offline, saved map, routing message, demo). Keep danger first and the others below.
- The Group page shows both "Invite code DUB026" text and an "Invite people" button. The text is useful (it can be read aloud), so keep it, but it's secondary.
- The emergency headline at 44pt is good. The Warning and Watch headlines at 28pt match screen titles, which is acceptable.

## Questions to Consider

- What does the app look like at minute 10 of an earthquake, after the alert modal is gone? That's the screen people will actually be looking at.
- If a family member presses "I need help", how many seconds until everyone else in the group notices?
- Should drills look visibly different from real alerts everywhere, so practice can never be mistaken for the real thing?
